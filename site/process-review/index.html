Who it is for

A process review suits firms where skilled people lose hours to repetitive, document-heavy work, and where it is not yet clear whether AI would help. It might begin with sorting incoming enquiries, organising client documents as they arrive, preparing summaries that link back to their sources, or drafting routine replies. These are examples, not a menu: we begin with the process you name. There are more starting points in who we help.

The problem it addresses

AI tools are easy to find and hard to judge. Before spending on one, most firms need answers they do not yet have. Is this process worth improving? What does it cost today, in time and in mistakes? Which information would an AI tool see, and is that acceptable? Who would check the result?

A review answers those questions for one process, from how the work is actually done, so that any later decision rests on more than a demonstration.

What happens

A review follows the first steps of our approach.

  1. Observe the existing process. We look at the work as it is actually done, not only as the procedure describes it.
  2. Establish a baseline. How often it happens, how long it takes and where it goes wrong, so there is something honest to compare against.
  3. Agree what information and tools may be used. Clear boundaries for sensitive information, settled before anything is built.
  4. Make a recommendation. We tell you whether AI is worth trying in this workflow and, if so, what a pilot would cover and where a person must review the result.

What you receive

A focused recommendation and a scope for a pilot. The recommendation may be that AI is not worth using for this workflow, or not yet. That is a useful result, and you can stop there. What the review includes, and what it does not, is set out in the written fee you receive before any work starts.

Safeguards

  • Please keep confidential client information and sensitive personal data out of your first enquiry. Our privacy notice explains how we handle what you send.
  • Which information and which tools the work may use is agreed with you before anything is built.
  • Sensitive information, accuracy and human accountability matter at every step, not just at the end.

Limits

  • A review covers one workflow, not the whole firm.
  • It does not build or install anything. Building and testing is the one-workflow pilot.
  • It does not promise a result. The outcomes we aim for are things to investigate and measure with you.
  • We do not sell a catalogue of AI tools. The recommendation starts from your process, not from a product.

Cost

Between £500 and £750, once, excluding VAT. Where the fee falls depends on how many people, tools and exceptions the workflow involves. You get a written fee before any work starts, and each stage is a separate decision. All three prices are in the costs section.

Next step

Describe one process that takes too much time or causes friction, using the form on the contact section of the home page. We reply to arrange an initial conversation, and we tell you plainly whether there is a useful fit and what a first step could look like. No pressure, and no obligation.
