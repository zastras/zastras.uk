Who it is for

A pilot suits a firm that knows which workflow it wants to improve, usually because a process review has shown where AI could help and what the pilot should cover. It is for one workflow, not a rollout across the firm.

The problem it addresses

An idea that looks good in a demonstration may not hold up on your own work, which includes the difficult cases: an unusual request, a missing document, an unclear instruction. A pilot tests an improvement on representative normal and difficult examples before anyone relies on it, and compares the result with the original process, so the decision to carry on is made on evidence.

What happens

  1. Build and test a focused workflow. One workflow, tested on representative normal and difficult examples.
  2. Set human review and escalation points. Who checks what, and what happens when the answer is unclear.
  3. Measure the result. We compare the pilot with the original process, using the baseline from the review. Depending on the workflow, that could be minutes per task, how often drafts need correcting, or response times against your own standards.
  4. Review the exceptions and adjust. What went wrong teaches us as much as what went right.

As an illustration only: a tenant reports a leaking tap by email. The workflow prepares a category, an urgency and a suggested reply, shown beside the original message. A member of staff checks, edits or rejects the draft before anything is sent. The approved reply goes out and the job is logged. Every real workflow starts from the firm’s own process, tools and obligations.

What you receive

A tested workflow, compared with how you work today, with the review and escalation points that go with it. What the pilot includes, and what it does not, is set out in the written fee you receive before any work starts.

Safeguards

  • People stay in control of important outputs. A person reviews what matters before anything goes out.
  • Which information and which tools the workflow may use is agreed with you before it is built.
  • Please keep confidential client information and sensitive personal data out of your first enquiry. Our privacy notice explains how we handle what you send.

Limits

  • A pilot covers one workflow and is tightly scoped. It shows whether the workflow is worth using; it is not a rollout.
  • It may show that the workflow should not go ahead, or should change. That is a result, not a failure.
  • It does not promise time saved or any other result. The comparison with your baseline is the evidence.
  • Monitoring, exception reviews and agreed changes once a workflow is in everyday use are separate. We suggest a retainer only when there is real ongoing work, and we will say so if a handover is enough. See the retainer terms.

Cost

Between £2,000 and £5,000, once, excluding VAT. The price follows the scope we agree after the review, and we keep that scope tight so the result can be tested, understood and trusted. You get a written fee before any work starts, and each stage is a separate decision. All three prices are in the costs section.

If you have not yet had a process review, that is the usual place to start: see what a process review involves. Or describe one process that takes too much time or causes friction, using the form in the contact section of the home page, and we will tell you plainly whether there is a useful fit.
